02 — ANALYSIS

Speed vs. Accuracy Trade-off Analysis

Graph RAG — Best Overall Balance (Recommended)

Graph RAG achieves the highest performance across all five metrics with a decisive margin. Its Hit Rate of 0.8268 indicates that in 82.7% of questions at least one correct article appears in the top-3 retrieved sources. Its MRR of 0.6719 shows that when it retrieves a correct article, it ranks it first or second on average. These retrieval strengths directly support its highest Legal Accuracy (0.3060). The primary cost is inference latency: Graph RAG responses average 152 seconds per query (observed range: 101–226 seconds), roughly 2.2× slower than Qwen RAG and 1.6× slower than CamELBERT RAG. This latency is attributable to graph traversal overhead (PageRank + graph_score computation per source). For batch/offline evaluation tasks, Graph RAG is the clear recommendation. For low-latency production deployments, its latency requires mitigation (pre-computed subgraphs, caching).

Qwen RAG — Best Speed/Quality Balance for Production

Qwen RAG achieves 74.3% of Graph RAG's Hit Rate (0.6142 vs 0.8268) and 74.3% of its MRR (0.5000 vs 0.6719) while delivering answers at approximately 100 seconds per query on average — faster than Graph RAG. Its Legal Accuracy (0.2938) is only marginally below Graph RAG (−0.0122). Critically, Qwen RAG has the fewest total failures below the 0.3 Legal Accuracy threshold (62 vs 67 for Graph RAG and 90 for CamELBERT), suggesting its answer text quality is comparatively more stable even when retrieval partially fails. For production systems where latency is a constraint, Qwen RAG represents the best speed-to-accuracy ratio.

CamELBERT RAG — Not Production-Ready (Retrieval Collapse)

CamELBERT RAG exhibits a critical retrieval failure: with a Hit Rate of 0.2520, it fails to retrieve any correct article in 74.8% of questions. Its MRR of 0.1916 confirms that even when a correct article is retrieved, it is rarely ranked first. The root cause appears to be a semantic mismatch between the Arabic embedding space of CamELBERT and the specific legal register of Algerian law — the model systematically confuses article numbers and retrieves semantically adjacent but legally wrong sources (e.g., retrieving Civil Code articles for Penal Code questions, or Labor Law articles for Administrative Law questions). Despite having the fastest observed latency (~80–100 seconds), speed offers no advantage when retrieval correctness is this low. CamELBERT RAG requires re-indexing with domain-adapted embeddings before being suitable for legal RAG.

04 — METHODOLOGY

Evaluation Methodology

Computation Engine

All retrieval metrics (Hit Rate, Precision, Recall, MRR) were computed deterministically in Python against 127 ground-truth questions. Article matching used normalized Arabic text: tashkeel removal, Alef variant unification (أ/إ/آ → ا), Yaa/Alef Maqsura normalization (ى → ي), and Taa Marbuta normalization (ة → ه). Matching allowed substring containment in addition to exact equality to handle minor formatting differences across sources.

Legal Accuracy was computed via a four-factor weighted composite: (1) Article number F1-score between answer and ground truth (45%), (2) Penalty fact overlap — monetary amounts, imprisonment durations, legal consequence keywords (30%), (3) Law domain accuracy (15%), (4) Retrieved source-GT overlap (10%). Refusal responses (explicit statements of "no relevant article found") were capped at 0.20. All scores are bounded [0.0, 1.0].

Failure threshold: Legal Accuracy < 0.3 (absolute). Top 3 failures selected by ascending Legal Accuracy sort per model.
